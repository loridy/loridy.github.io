ML for Stock Selection

Comparing models, signal horizons and feature sets without confusing classification accuracy with tradable alpha.

01 / Research question

Do fundamental and macro variables add meaningful out-of-sample predictive information beyond price and volume features when predicting the direction of US equity returns?

The underlying public project is titled Next-Gen Stock Selection: A Comparative Study of Machine Learning Models for US Market Forecasting.

02 / Experimental design

DimensionDocumented approach
UniverseUS equities: pilot subset, aiming for broader S&P 500 coverage
TargetsForward direction over 1, 3, 5 and 10 trading days
FeaturesA: technical; B: technical + fundamentals; C: technical + fundamentals + macro
ModelsLogistic regression benchmark and tree-based alternatives
TestsTime-aware split, balanced accuracy, F1 and confusion matrix

03 / What can go wrong?

A model that predicts “up” frequently may enjoy superficially attractive accuracy in a rising market without forecasting any tradable edge. Balanced accuracy and an always-up baseline are therefore central to the experiment.

  • Point-in-time fundamentals and release-date alignment matter more than a large feature count.
  • Using current constituent lists for historical tests can cause survivorship bias.
  • Labels at nearby horizons overlap; naive confidence intervals can overstate certainty.
  • Model selection on the test period undermines the validity of out-of-sample evaluation.

04 / From predictive power to alpha

This is a model-comparison research project; classification performance is not the same as portfolio alpha. A separate investment strategy would require signal sizing, realistic costs, turnover, drawdown and exposure controls. No unsupported model performance numbers are published here.
